Experience in WoW Forever works much like Classic, with the same amount needed per level and the same cap at 60. What changed are the sources: dungeons pay differently, green mobs give less, and camps, food and the Cozy Sleeping Bag add bonuses on top. This guide covers it all.

## How experience works

You earn experience from quests, kills, exploration and dungeons. With enough of it you reach the next level, up to the cap of 60. There you stop gaining experience.

The experience bar at the bottom of the screen shows how far you are. Hover over it to see your current experience and how much you still need. Every gain also appears in the chat.

## Quests

A quest gives experience based on its level, its type and how hard its objectives are. The quest log shows the amount before you accept. Quests with slow or tough objectives usually pay more than others of the same level.

The quest log colors every quest by its level compared to yours:

- **Gray:** 5 or more levels below you
- **Green:** 3 to 4 levels below
- **Yellow:** up to 2 levels above or below
- **Orange:** 3 to 4 levels above
- **Red:** 5 or more levels above

For quests more than 5 levels below you, the experience drops by 10 to 20% for every extra level.

**Elite and group quests** send you after stronger monsters or into dangerous places. You often need help, and the reward is higher, experience included.

**Repeatable quests** have a blue marker instead of a yellow one. Most give no experience, so do them only for the other rewards.

## Kills

Monsters give experience based on their level compared to yours and on their type. Monsters close to your level give the best balance between speed and experience. Much lower ones give little, much higher ones take too long.

Nameplates use the same colors as the quest log. Gray monsters give no experience at all. Monsters with a skull are far above your level and best avoided.

**Green mobs give less than in Classic.** Monsters below your level pay less in Forever, from a few percent to about a quarter per kill. Blizzard says that is [intended](/news/green-mobs-give-less-experience-than-in-classic/), to balance the new buffs from food and camps. Monsters of your own level give exactly as much as in Classic.

**Elites** have a golden dragon around their portrait. They give more experience than normal monsters, but take so much longer that they are rarely worth farming.

### Tagging and kill credit

Only the first player or group to hit a monster, with damage or a debuff, gets the experience. A monster tagged by someone else gets a gray nameplate. In a group, every member who is alive and nearby gets credit, so you do not each need to hit every monster.

### AoE grinding

Pulling a pack and burning it down with area spells can be fast in the right spot. Blizzard made it less effective in Forever, especially for Mages, who were known for it in Classic. It still works to some degree with the right spells, gear or help. You need a way to survive the pack or keep it away with roots, slows and stuns, plenty of monsters and few players competing for them.

## Dungeons

Dungeon experience changed several times on the beta. In September Blizzard [moved experience from kills to quests](/news/wow-forever-dungeon-experience-changes/): monsters gave little, the dungeon quests a lot. On 1 October the extra experience from dungeon quests was [cut by 50%](/news/dungeon-quests-give-50-percent-less-bonus-experience/). A week later dungeon monsters got [20% more experience](/news/dungeon-kills-give-20-percent-more-experience/), and a bug that based every kill on the highest level in the group was fixed.

The best approach is to pick up the dungeon quests in advance and complete them during the run. Pulling large packs and killing them with area spells, called dungeon cleave, also works, but Blizzard does not want dungeons to be the fastest road to 60.

A much higher player in the group costs a lot of experience. A level 60 clearing a dungeon for you is no longer an efficient way to level. Group with players in your own level range. The [dungeon guide](/guides/dungeons/) lists every dungeon with its levels and quests.

## Exploration

Entering a new area for the first time gives a little experience and reveals it on your map. The amount is small, so it is not worth a detour, but it adds up while you travel for quests.

## Groups

In a group, the experience per kill depends on the size of the group and the levels of its members. A bigger group means less per player, and a much higher member cuts it sharply. In return you kill faster and finish quests more easily. Quest rewards do not change in a group, but quest objectives do not count in a raid group.

## Rested experience

Rested XP doubles the experience from kills until it runs out. Quests and exploration do not get the bonus.

You build it up in an inn, a capital city or in the Cozy Sleeping Bag. Your portrait then shows a ZZZ. Log out there and it keeps building while you are offline. With rested XP, the filled part of your experience bar turns blue instead of purple, and a marker shows how far the rested pool reaches.

<img src="/guides/experience/rested-portrait.png" alt="A Night Elf portrait at level 10 with the ZZZ of a rested area, a screenshot from the beta of World of Warcraft: Forever" width="90" height="100" loading="lazy">

| | |
|---|---|
| Speed | 5% of your current level per 8 hours in a rested area |
| Cap | 150% of your current level, 30 bubbles |
| From empty to full | About 10 days logged out |
| Legacy: Well Rested | 4% faster and a 4% higher cap per rank, up to 20% |

**Well Rested** raises the speed and the cap by the same percentage. Filling up still takes about 10 days, but the pool holds up to a fifth more. **Talented**, the next Legacy talent, gives your first talent point up to five levels earlier. Both are in the [Legacy calculator](/talents/legacy/).

## Camps, food and the sleeping bag

Forever adds three bonuses that stack with each other and with rested XP.

**Camp Tent.** At a camp, a Leatherworker can set up a Camp Tent. Resting there gives rested experience as in an inn, up to 5% of a level. The buff lasts an hour and stays when you die. The [camping guide](/guides/camping/) covers every camp object.

**Well Fed.** Most food from Cooking gives Well Fed, with 5% more experience from kills on top of its other stats. It usually lasts about 15 minutes and is lost when you die. Cooking is a secondary profession, so it comes on top of your two primary ones, and you need it to light a campfire.

**Cozy Sleeping Bag.** Roll it out anywhere and lie down for up to 3 stacks of Well-Rested, 1% more experience per stack. The buff stays when you die, and lying in the bag counts as a rested area, also while logged out. The [sleeping bag guide](/guides/cozy-sleeping-bag/) walks through the quest chain across both continents.
